# Investigation 01 — Entry and first reconstruction

## Question

What procedure is evidenced by the translation project's initial workflow declaration and its first retrospective reconstruction checkpoint, and what must remain unresolved until the developmental record is examined further?

## Evidence and provenance

### Earliest recoverable project intent

A retrospective search of prior conversation summaries identifies the initial request on 2026-11-05 at 21:51 UTC: establish a structured English translation branch that builds understanding while preserving the Chinese source and tracing English interpretations back to it.

The same early conversation records several constraints and clarifications:

- The skills-development project was to be used as an informing methodological reference, not imposed as the target architecture.
- An alternate observer position was requested. Domain concerns were to remain legitimate; unfamiliarity with the domain was not to be treated as evidence of deficiency.
- Skill-level reconstruction and methodological learning were to remain distinct.
- Phase 0 was accepted at 2026-11-06 00:02 UTC: establish a baseline and inventory before reconstruction or translation, explicitly separating source statements, inferences and uncertainties.

This is a secondary retrospective account derived from conversation-search summaries, not a recovered verbatim transcript. It establishes the earliest currently recoverable intent and constraints, but not the full dialogue or every intervening decision. The original conversation remains a provenance gap.

### Translation workflow declaration

The translation README describes a proposed workflow with six phases:

1. Establish the source baseline and inventory.
2. Reconstruct the skill's structure.
3. Build a controlled glossary.
4. Translate in dependency order.
5. Perform technical and semantic review.
6. Validate the whole skill.

It also declares working principles: source before interpretation, understand before rewriting, preserve operational force, keep technical tokens exact, trace translations, separate translation from correction, preserve provenance/licence notices, and validate the skill as a system.

The document calls these phases proposed. It is evidence of the workflow as declared in the translation project, not proof that every phase was followed in that order or that the workflow captures all operational movements.

### Inventory

The inventory explicitly characterises itself as a baseline inventory, not a translation, technical audit or redesign. It records repository identity, source revision, file coverage, existing English material and an initial document-role map.

Its later progress section preserves subsequent status changes. This means the inventory is both a baseline artifact and an evolving progress surface; those roles should be distinguished when reconstructing chronology.

### Pass 1 / Checkpoint 01

The checkpoint identifies itself as a retrospective session-ingress record. It explicitly states that it preserves the observer position recoverable at the end of the session and does not claim that every item was recorded synchronously when first observed.

It establishes two outputs that must not be conflated:

- skill-level reconstruction: understanding how the skill organises work, decisions, evidence, experience and handoff;
- methodological learning: observing what the reconstruction may reveal about skill development, organisation, composition and validation.

It positions the observer differently from a domain specialist: the immediate task is to understand the skill's structure, operation, document relationships and language before assessing technical truth. It does not dismiss domain expertise.

It defines Pass 1 as an initial structural and linguistic reconstruction, not a technical audit or redesign. It distinguishes a complete traversal pass from session checkpoints and document positions.

It also preserves source/interpretation boundaries, unresolved questions, a bounded reconstruction-sufficiency rule, and cold-start success criteria.

## First reconstructed movements

The records support the following initial movement, with different evidentiary status:

1. **Establish a source baseline.** Record repository identity, source revision, tree and file inventory before treating the material as a coherent system.
2. **Establish an observer position.** State what is being investigated and distinguish skill reconstruction from technical validation and methodological learning.
3. **Recover structure before rewriting.** Identify document roles, dependencies, inputs, outputs, decision logic, evidence and completion criteria.
4. **Preserve terminology as a controlled distinction.** Build a glossary from contextual operational roles rather than natural-sounding equivalence.
5. **Translate in dependency order.** Use the recovered structure to determine a workable order, while allowing source dependencies to change that order.
6. **Keep translation and correction separate.** Preserve source ambiguities and technical questions rather than silently resolving them in English.
7. **Review at more than one level.** Compare individual source/translation pairs, then consider whole-skill coherence and usability.
8. **Preserve a reconstruction record separately from the functional translation.** The reconstruction record carries source provenance, observer understanding, relationships, ambiguities and boundaries; the translation is the English functional expression.
9. **Make continuation reconstructable.** Preserve the current pass, checkpoint, document position, rationale for the next hop, reading dependencies and cold-start success conditions.

This is a first reconstruction from the workflow declaration and checkpoint evidence. It is not yet a chronological account of when each movement first appeared or how each was modified.

## Pass 1: movement within a declared traversal

The Pass 1 checkpoints show a repeated operational form across the experience-library traversal:

- preserve the Pass identity and purpose;
- identify the completed source and its source revision;
- identify the corresponding English translation, review record and Chinese descriptive reconstruction;
- record the next source and why it is the next adequate hop;
- distinguish source-explicit links from collection-level references and observer-reconstructed correspondences;
- preserve unresolved issues and technical-validation boundaries;
- record the re-entry sequence for the next session;
- state the position change without changing the Pass identity.

The sequence is not merely a list of completed documents. Checkpoints 02–11 preserve why the next source is selected, often by an explicit source link, a collection index, or a relationship that the next document can independently confirm or disconfirm. The previous document's interpretation is not to be imposed on the next one.

Checkpoint 07 contains a bounded-sufficiency statement: the material required for the current structural/linguistic purpose had been recovered; remaining questions required new evidence sources or a separate validation scope and did not block moving to the next source. It also states conditions for reopening a question, such as new discriminating evidence, a relevant observer-position change, a translation conflict or a concrete interpretive consequence.

Checkpoint 12 does not treat reaching the last experience-library document as proof that Pass 1 is complete. It directs a scope reconciliation against the inventory and original repository tree. The subsequent scope-reconciliation record declares Pass 1 complete only for the defined operational core and explicitly excludes technical validation, whole-skill operational validation, and remaining repository-facing/integration/legal/asset work.

This is evidence of a local traversal being bounded by its declared purpose and scope, rather than by exhaustion of all possible questions.

## Pass boundaries: changed observer position and purpose

The records show three distinct passes, not one continuous document-by-document progression:

- **Pass 1 — initial structural and linguistic reconstruction:** recover the operational core's structure, roles, relationships and evidence boundaries without conducting technical validation.
- **Pass 2 — repository-facing English material:** change observer position to the public reader surface and review the Chinese README against its existing English counterpart. Its scope is deliberately bounded to that pair; its closure record does not silently absorb QR assets, marketplace metadata or external compatibility.
- **Pass 3 — technical and semantic review:** return to the operational document set with a different question: whether the English translation preserves imperative force, conditional scope, decision logic, technical tokens, structure, cross-document references and uncertainty. The Pass 3 declaration explicitly excludes proving technical claims against upstream implementations or real devices.

Each pass declares its purpose, scope and exit condition. The next pass is not an automatic continuation of the previous pass's document position. It is a new traversal from a changed observer position.

## First reconstructed movements (expanded)

Taken together, the evidence currently supports this provisional movement sequence:

1. Establish a source baseline before transformation.
2. Choose an observer position that can inspect the skill without confusing domain expertise with the immediate reconstruction task.
3. Recover the source structure and relationships before translating.
4. Preserve terminology and distinctions as controlled decisions.
5. Translate in dependency order, retaining source traceability.
6. Separate translation from correction and source fidelity from technical truth.
7. Preserve a distinct descriptive reconstruction and translation-review provenance surface.
8. Traverse a declared scope through bounded document-level hops.
9. Preserve position, rationale, unresolved questions and re-entry conditions at checkpoints.
10. Reconcile the completed traversal against its declared scope rather than inferring closure from the last item.
11. Start a new pass when the purpose or observer position changes, and compare it against prior baselines.

These movements are not yet asserted to be universal invariants. Some may be specific to translation, some may have been introduced by the translation project, and some may have emerged through AI-assisted collaboration.

## Important distinctions

- Declared workflow is not identical to enacted procedure.
- A retrospective checkpoint is not contemporaneous evidence of every earlier decision.
- Structural reconstruction is not technical correctness validation.
- Translation is not correction.
- Pass number is not session number or document position.
- Skill-level reconstruction and methodological learning are distinct outputs, even when they inform one another.
- A record's creation date is not necessarily the date of the observation it reconstructs.
- A bounded pass closure is not completeness of the overall investigation.
- A later pass is not simply a continuation from the previous pass's last document.
